The charter

At Amazon Fresh, I owned the product charter for our inventory visibility and diagnostics platform. As part of that charter, I proposed and led a net-new 0-to-1 initiative called UARC2, a supply chain visibility and failure-diagnostics system that identifies true causational relationships and pinpoints exactly which events drive stockouts.

Finding the opportunity

To start the product development lifecycle, I gathered user feedback on the biggest opportunities across our supply chain and identified our stockout-attribution tooling as the highest-value one. Researching the existing solution, I saw it only surfaced correlations preceding stockouts, not causes. I formed a hypothesis: if we installed additional instrumentation along the supply chain, we could decompose the entire physical and virtual operation into a connected set of links, and isolate true causation instead of correlation. I pitched our science lead on a prototype, ran an experiment at a single fulfillment center, and the results were promising enough that I built a data-backed proposal to leadership.

Execution

We won approval and funding, and I executed development over the next 12 months. I ran a daily standup with our engineering and science team to drive delivery, a weekly tactical sync with my IC and first-line-manager partners to resolve open questions fast, and a biweekly sync with my Senior Manager and Director stakeholders for hot topics and strategic trade-offs. Later in development, I demoed the product to Amazon VP-level leadership, which won additional funding for the following year's roadmap.

Once live, the system let us trace a single stockout back to its true root cause, whether external, like a crop failure in a strawberry field, or internal, like a bug in how our forecast-and-buying system handled frequent-reorder, long-lead-time products, so teams could react strategically instead of guessing.

The hard part

One of the harder parts of the project was aligning senior stakeholders on strategic priorities. I had seven functional customer orgs, each wanting its own features prioritized, which created real friction early in roadmap planning. I defused it by aligning the team around an objective prioritization framework that weighed actionability alongside raw impact to order the epics, so tradeoffs were decided by shared criteria rather than by whoever pushed hardest. I also stayed close to the Senior Managers and Directors, and brought in ICs and first-line managers from their orgs for a weekly working sync. Involving those operators directly earned trust, kept their leaders tightly looped in, and turned prioritization from a negotiation into a collaboration. This process built customer buy-in and insulated the product team from roadmap thrash, letting us ship the highest-priority items on schedule and deliver for our customers.

Outcome

We launched in January 2026. UARC2 proved 4x more accurate than the system it replaced, reached 92% adoption as the default decision layer for our instock and supply-chain teams, and lifted instock, our NSM, by up to 200 bps in some grocery categories.
