Ground Transportation · A Point of View

Twelve handoffs, and nobody owns the middle.

A truck move is a chain of handoffs between parties with misaligned incentives and no shared system of record. The receiver controls the appointment and bears none of the cost of a bad one. The carrier bears all of it and controls nothing. This is what I have come to understand about ground freight, and where I think the leverage is.

Start with the lifecycle
PILLAR 01

The ground freight lifecycle, and where it breaks

Two lanes running left to right. The top lane is the physical movement of the freight. The bottom lane is the information, the money, and the verification that has to move alongside it. The interesting part is where those two lanes fall out of step.

Interactive lifecycle diagram goes here

Twelve steps, two swim lanes, hover-to-expand pain markers. Empty for now.

  • The twelve steps. Quote, tender, accept or reject, cover, dispatch, pickup appointment, in transit, delivery appointment, POD, invoice, settlement, claims.
  • Two lanes. Physical custody on top, informational and financial underneath, so the reader can see where custody changes hands but the data does not.
  • Pain markers. Roughly ten, of which three are visually loud and the rest are quiet. A wall of equally weighted markers reads as noise.
  • Each marker expands to: what the pain is, who feels it, frequency and cost, what people do about it today, whether Flexport touches it, and whether it is an AI opportunity or a human-plus-agent opportunity.
  • The annotation that makes it a PM artifact: at each step, who controls the decision versus who bears the cost. That inversion is the actual mechanism behind most of these failures, and no other candidate will have drawn it.
