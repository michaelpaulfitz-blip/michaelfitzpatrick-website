Catalog Lab: catalog pain points and a working gluten-free evidence engine

I built this after my conversations with the catalog team. The pain points gather what came up across those conversations, grouped by theme, with real-world approaches for each. The lab collects gluten-free evidence from independent public sources, scores every product with provenance on each claim, and measures precision and recall at any threshold. The photo reader tests whether package images can act as one more source. The diagrams show the demo architecture next to my current picture of Instacart's catalog.

Method and limits

How this was built

What the lab uses, what it assumes, and what I would do next.

Sources

  • Open Food Facts through its documented API, as an identified client within the published rate limit.
  • USDA FoodData Central branded foods.
  • The GFCO public product finder for certification, matched by UPC.
  • No Instacart data, and nothing collected from Instacart's site. The Kroger product API is wired in and waiting on keys, so the same checks can run on a retail partner's feed.

The sample

  • 402 products: twelve gluten-free and twelve regular in each of 13 categories, extra oats, and every genuine conflict found in a wider sweep.
  • The sample is enriched for conflicts on purpose. Its rates show what the engine catches and are not catalog rates. Each headline number states its frame.

Rules and assumptions

  • Verified means a GFCO listing matched by UPC. Other certifiers are not checked yet, and a listing under a similar name with a different UPC carries no weight.
  • Implied wheat counts as gluten: bare flour, plain soy sauce, bread crumbs, beer and ale. This rule is the likeliest source of a false conflict.
  • Confidence weights are set by hand today. They should be fit to a hand-labeled gold set.
  • Until that gold set exists, precision and recall are measured against Claude Haiku pre-labels, three votes per product, 145 of 150 unanimous.

What I would do next

  • Label a gold set by hand, ideally with a second labeler so agreement can be reported.
  • Add the other certifiers and the Kroger partner feed.
  • Extend to dairy-free and the major allergens. The detector and scorer take new families as new term lists and certifier sources.
