Gear guide · Owner: Mike

Car seat, stroller & monitor

What reviewers and parents say, what to look for, and ranked picks. Priorities: a light car seat and stroller (they'll get carried a lot) without skimping on quality, and a feature-rich monitor.

Recommended setup

  • Nuna PIPA rx + UPPAbaby Minu V3 (with adapter): about 8.5 lb + 16.7 lb, both best in their class, roughly $1,100–1,200 total.
  • Want no adapter? Nuna TRIV next travel system. Weight matters most? Swap in the PIPA aire rx (about 7 lb).
  • Monitor: Nanit, plus a ~$77 VTech audio unit as an offline backup. If you'd rather skip subscriptions, get the eufy E21.
  • Before buying: test-push strollers up a hill at a Seattle store, and check the car seat's manufacture date (see the safety notes below).

Infant car seat: top 5

Weights are for the carrier only. The first number is the maker's spec; the second is BabyGearLab's as-used measurement, which includes the canopy and insert.

  1. 1Nuna PIPA rx7.6 / 8.5 lb · ~$550 Best crash score BabyGearLab has tested (9.0), with a load leg, anti-rebound bar, rigid LATCH and European belt path. Fits more strollers than any other seat here. Downside: you have to rethread the harness to adjust it.
  2. 2Nuna PIPA aire rx6.2 / 7.1 lb · $440–550 Nearly the same crash score (8.7) at about 1.4 lb lighter. The catch: it fits fewer strollers, and installing it with the seat belt is harder. Recline: the US version comes with the RELX base, which has a 4-position push-button recline and an angle indicator (see the video note below).
  3. 3UPPAbaby Aria V26.0 / 6.8 lb · $400–450 The lightest seat available, with a load leg and anti-rebound panel. Effectively only works with UPPAbaby strollers.
  4. 4Clek Liing9 / 10.5 lb · $500–550 Consumer Reports Expert Pick with excellent install hardware, and the narrowest seat here. About 2–3 lb heavier than the Nunas.
  5. 5Cybex Aton G2 / G2 Swivel~9 lb · $400–550 Load leg and European belt path. Less independently tested than the others. The older Aton G had a 2025 recall; the G2 hasn't been recalled.

The Snuggle Bugz video

  • "Best Infant Car Seat 2026": compares 7 seats on handle, canopy, fit, fabric, recline, base and install, then picks a top 3.
  • We couldn't get a transcript, so its winners aren't reflected here. Its lineup overlaps ours: Nuna PIPA, Clek Liing and Romer Juni. The Juni crash-tests well (8.8) but weighs 10–11 lb.
  • It's a Canadian store, so some of the models it shows differ from the US versions.
  • Its "no recline or leveling" knock on the PIPA doesn't match the US seat. The US PIPA aire rx and rx ship with the RELX base: 4 recline positions (two for 4–20 lb, two for 21–32 lb), a push button you can use after the base is installed, and an angle indicator. Canadian PIPAs have been sold without a load leg, with a single level line, and with a rolled towel needed to fix over-recline, which is likely what the video is describing. The carrier itself never reclines; on infant seats the base sets the angle.

What to look for

  • Carrier weight: under 8 lb is light. Maker specs leave out the canopy and insert, which add about 1 lb.
  • Load leg + anti-rebound bar: both reduce how much the seat moves in a crash. Rigid LATCH makes it easier to install correctly.
  • European belt path: gives a solid install without the base, which matters for Ubers.
  • Babies usually outgrow these seats by height (30–32 in) before weight.
  • A no-rethread harness is a nice-to-have. Fabric without flame retardants is available from Nuna, UPPAbaby, Clek and Chicco ClearTex.

Also good / skip

  • Budget: Joie Rue (~$170, ~7.5 lb) or Britax Willow S (~$250).
  • Skip: Doona (17 lb, lowest crash score at 4.6), UPPAbaby Mesa V3 (crash score 5.1), Cybex Cloud T (12 lb).
  • Never import an EU-only seat such as the Bugaboo Turtle Air. It isn't certified to US standards.

Safety notes

  • New side-impact standard: it applies to seats made on or after Dec 5, 2026. Black Friday stock will mostly be older. That's still legal, but check the date sticker.
  • 2025–26 recalls: Cybex Aton G (made 2023–24), the Britax B-Safe line, Graco SnugRide Turn & Slide, and Evenflo LiteMax. None on the PIPA seats, Aria V2, Liing or KeyFit.

Why it's still the pick

  • Second-lightest seat on the market (about 7 lb in use). Only the Aria V2 is lighter, and that one locks you into UPPAbaby strollers.
  • Nearly the best crash results anywhere: 8.7, against 9.0 for the heavier rx.
  • All the premium safety hardware: load leg, anti-rebound, rigid LATCH.
  • Nicest fabrics in the PIPA line, plus magnetic buckle holders that keep the straps out of your way when you put the baby in.
  • Recline: the RELX base has 4 positions with a push button that works after it's installed, plus an angle indicator. The video's "no recline" complaint doesn't apply to the US version.

What to weigh

  • Price: about $100 more than the PIPA rx, which is 1.4 lb heavier.
  • Without the base (Uber, travel), installing it with the seat belt is harder than with the rx.
  • Stroller fit: before buying the Minu V3 adapter, confirm it lists the aire rx.
  • Height limit: sources conflict, 30 in or 32 in. Babies usually outgrow these seats by height, so check Nuna's spec.
  • Canopy and insert add about 1–2 lb. The in-use weight is still lighter than almost everything else.
  • New side-impact rule: it applies to seats made from Dec 5, 2026. Check the date sticker.

Two cars?

  • Yes, extra bases are very common. One seat, one base per car. The seat clicks into whichever car you're taking.
  • Extra RELX base: about $250. Worth it if both cars regularly carry the baby, for example if either of us might do daycare drop-off.
  • Occasional second car: skip the second base and install the carrier with the seat belt instead. It works, but it's slower on the aire rx.
  • Avoid swapping one base between cars. Every reinstall is another chance to get it wrong.
  • Install each base in its car, check the angle indicator, and consider a free car seat technician check (Safe Kids WA).

Every feature worth comparing

Safety

  • Crash test results (BabyGearLab, Consumer Reports)
  • Load leg and anti-rebound bar: both reduce how much the seat moves in a crash
  • Side-impact protection; whether it meets the new FMVSS 213a rule
  • Recall history
  • Expiration date: usually 6–7 years after manufacture

Install

  • Rigid LATCH vs. strap LATCH
  • Recline positions and an easy-to-read angle indicator
  • Installing without the base: European belt path
  • Belt lock-off on the base, for cars without LATCH
  • Fit in our cars: width and how much front-seat legroom it takes

Everyday use

  • Carrier weight, both listed and in use
  • Handle comfort and how easily it releases
  • Harness: no-rethread, buckle holders
  • Canopy size; UPF rating; privacy drape
  • Click-in and release from both the base and the stroller

Comfort & materials

  • Fabric free of flame retardants
  • Breathability (merino, mesh)
  • Newborn insert and the minimum baby weight (4 lb on most)
  • Machine-washable cover

System & cost

  • Strollers it fits and which adapters it needs
  • Extra base price, if we use two cars
  • FAA approval for flights
  • Height and weight limits: babies usually outgrow these by height
  • Resale value and warranty

Stroller: top 5

Ranked for a light stroller that can still handle Seattle hills and rain.

  1. 1UPPAbaby Minu V316.7 lb · $500–600 The only stroller under 17 lb that reviewers say works as your everyday main stroller. Seat lies flat from birth, big basket, #1 at BabyGearLab and a CR 2026 Top Pick. The rain shield costs extra.
  2. 2Bugaboo Dragonfly Plus~22 lb · ~$1,349 The lightest stroller with a real bassinet that folds with the frame, which is good for long rainy-season walks. The Clek Liing clicks in directly.
  3. 3Nuna TRIV next + PIPA18.5 lb · ~$1,350 bundle Best light travel system: the car seat adapter is included and the ride is smooth.
  4. 4Joolz Aer214.5 lb · ~$549 Lightest stroller that's genuinely usable from birth, with four-wheel suspension. Small basket, and its car seat adapters were recalled in June 2026.
  5. 5UPPAbaby Cruz V3~26 lb · ~$900 Best suspension and canopy for hills, and the rain shield is included. It's heavy, though, so you'd probably want a travel stroller too.

What to look for

  • Weight under 17 lb, and a one-hand fold that stands up on its own.
  • Wheels and suspension for rough sidewalks. Use a wrist strap on steep hills, since most of these have foot brakes.
  • Rain shield included? Yes on the Cruz and Vista; extra on the Minu and Butterfly 2.
  • From birth means a bassinet, a seat that lies flat, or a car seat that clicks in. Keep naps in the car seat short.
  • Resale: UPPAbaby, Bugaboo and Nuna hold their value well.

Pairing with the car seat

  • PIPA rx / aire rx: clicks straight into the Nuna TRVL lx. Ring adapter included with the TRIV, MIXX and SWIV. Fits the Minu, Cruz and Butterfly with an adapter.
  • Aria V2: UPPAbaby strollers only.
  • Clek Liing: clicks straight into the Bugaboo Dragonfly; needs an adapter for most others.
  • Chicco KeyFit: doesn't fit the Minu, Butterfly or Nuna strollers.

Also good / skip

  • Travel stroller: Joolz Aer2, or the Stokke YOYO3 if you want the tiniest fold. Skip a travel stroller entirely if the Minu is your main one.
  • Budget: Mockingbird Travel (~$399, 17.7 lb).
  • Skip: no-name strollers from Amazon and similar marketplaces. The CPSC issued stop-use warnings for some of them in 2025–26.

Baby monitor: features first

Plan: the baby room-shares in a bassinet at first, then moves to the nursery around 4–6 months.

  1. 1Nanit (+ Home display)$249–399 · plans optional ~$120–300/yr Best sleep analytics. Breathing-motion tracking, two-way audio, white noise and temperature/humidity are all free. Needs an overhead mount for the analytics. Depends on Wi-Fi and the cloud: an Oct 2025 outage lasted 18 hours.
  2. 2Owlet Dream Duo Gen 3~$380 · Owlet360 $99.99/yr optional The only FDA-cleared pulse and oxygen sock, paired with a 2K camera. Owners often report connection drops, and the readings can feed anxiety.
  3. 3eufy E21~$255 · no subscription 4K camera, a handheld parent unit that works without Wi-Fi plus a phone app, and local storage, so it's its own backup. No breathing tracking.
  4. 4Harbor~$499 10-inch parent unit, footage stored on the camera, keeps working without internet. Newer, less proven company.
  5. 5Philips Avent Premium Connected~$350 Shows breathing rate without a paywall, but it won't sound an alarm if breathing stops.

What to look for

  • Wi-Fi app vs. a dedicated parent unit: an app lets you check in remotely and gives you analytics. A dedicated non-Wi-Fi unit can't be hacked over the internet and works when the internet is down. Hybrid models do both.
  • Subscriptions: check what's free vs. paywalled before you buy.
  • Features to compare: cry detection, night vision, two-way audio, temperature/humidity, multi-camera, travel mount.
  • Backup: if you go Wi-Fi only, add a VTech DM1211 audio unit (~$77).

Breathing monitors

  • The AAP says consumer breathing monitors don't reduce SIDS risk and shouldn't replace safe-sleep practices. Using one on top of safe sleep is fine.
  • Owlet's sock is FDA-cleared for monitoring, not for preventing SIDS.

Skip

  • Miku: put previously free features behind a paywall in 2023. Lollipop: key features are paywalled.
  • Babysense Max View VBM55: recalled Feb 2026 (fire hazard).
  • No-name Wi-Fi cameras: a May 2026 flaw (Meari) exposed about 1.1M devices.
